Agtech fundraising / The founder’s outlook

2026 conditions · 2027 outlookGlobal funding · US foundersResearch cutoff: 30 October 2026

Don’t wait for the market
to make your raise easier.

There is money for agtech in 2026, but the figures do not show a broad recovery. The most likely 2027 scenario offers only modest improvement. Build your fundraising plan around what your company can prove, rather than a return of investor enthusiasm.

What is happening now?

Funding has settled at a much lower level.

Investment in farming, food production and related technologies was about half its peak in 2025. The adjusted 2026 figures give little reason to assume that the market has reopened. [1][5]

What is likely next?

2027 is more likely to be selective than easy.

The base case is $4–5.5bn of global agtech investment, with a 50% planning probability. That supports some companies’ growth; it does not imply easier access for every founder.

What should you do?

Make the next round about a result.

Show who buys, whether they buy again, and what it costs to deliver. Request the capital needed to prove the next part of the business, with room for fundraising to take longer.

The question to take into your next investor meeting: what will this round establish that makes the following round easier to justify?

01 / Is fundraising getting easier in 2026?

The headline has improved.
The market underneath it has not clearly improved.

PitchBook recorded $4.6bn of agtech investment in the first nine months of 2026. At that pace, the year would reach $6.1bn. But a single $1.2bn defence-focused drone round accounts for about a quarter of the money.

These are run rates: what a full year would look like if the observed pace continued. They are not final 2026 totals or forecasts. Removing one deal also does not remove every company that serves industries beyond agriculture.

Fewer deals are being recorded, but the decline needs careful comparison.

PitchBook’s Q3 2025 Agrifood VC First Look, published in October 2025, recorded 108 agtech deals. Its Q3 2026 Agtech VC First Look, published in October 2026, recorded 101. Comparing those initial quarterly counts gives a decline of about 6.5%. That comparison is more useful than comparing an incomplete current year with a more complete previous year. The original nine-month 2025 count has not been checked, so a comparable nine-month decline cannot be established. [4][5]

For your raise: treat a large funding headline as evidence that a particular company raised, not that your investor pool has expanded. Build the target list around investors currently able to fund your stage and business model.

02 / What gives a company a stronger funding case?

Connect the technology
to a purchase the customer can justify.

Investment is spread across genetics, software, equipment, food science and other businesses. Being in a funded category helps identify relevant investors. It does not establish demand for your product.

THE CUSTOMER’S REASON TO BUY

Put a value on the problem.

Identify the cost, loss or production limit the customer already faces. Explain how much your product changes it, under the conditions in which the customer operates.

THE EVIDENCE IT WORKS

Show results beyond the demonstration.

Bring field performance, paid use, repeat orders or renewals. Explain what still needs validation and what this round will resolve.

THE BUSINESS BEHIND THE PRODUCT

Include the full cost to deliver.

Installation, support, servicing and working capital belong in the economics. Sales growth is less persuasive if every new customer creates an unfunded obligation.

These are suggested investment criteria, not claims that every funded company meets the same criteria.

For a science-led company: technical novelty can help at seed, but it does not carry the whole funding case. AgFunder found a 78% seed-round premium for deeptech companies; it did not persist from Series B onward. Use early capital to build the commercial evidence the later investor will need. [3]

A product can save money
and still lose to a tighter budget.

Your customer’s ability to adopt is part of your funding case. Investors need to understand not only whether the technology pays back, but whether the buyer can fund it now.

04 / What could make 2027 better or worse?

Watch the money behind investors
and the cash behind customer demand.

The most likely 2027 scenario is a similar funding environment, with some room for improvement. Four conditions could change that forecast.

01 / INVESTORS RAISE NEW FUNDS

More money must reach your stage.

S2G closed a $1bn growth fund covering agriculture, energy and oceans. That is a meaningful pool of capital, but it is neither reserved entirely for agtech nor aimed at seed companies. The relevant question is how much newly raised capital can reach companies at your stage. [9]

What would help: more completed fundraises with budgets for companies at your stage. A fundraising target is not money ready to invest.

02 / EXITS RETURN CASH

Acquisitions must produce returns.

Deere bought Sentera and GUSS, but their prices were undisclosed. PitchBook recorded four agtech exits in Q3 2026. Buying useful technology and delivering strong returns to investors are different outcomes. [5][12][13]

What would help: cash returned to fund investors, giving them more reason and capacity to commit to the next fund.

03 / FINANCING GETS CHEAPER

Lower borrowing costs could support adoption.

The Fed held its policy rate at 3.75–4.00% in October. The base case assumes no automatic help from rate cuts. Customers’ actual loan rates and terms determine what they can finance. [8]

What would help: lower total financing costs and available credit, particularly for equipment purchases.

04 / CUSTOMER CASH FLOW IMPROVES

Better farm margins could unlock orders.

Pressure on farm income can delay purchases, renewals and expansion. Those delays then weaken the revenue evidence a startup brings to investors.

What would help: stronger margins and fewer postponed orders in your specific customer segment.

05 / The 2027 forecast

2027 is most likely to bring
another selective funding year.

The base forecast is $4–5.5bn of global agtech investment in 2027, with an estimated probability of 50%. The downside is $3–4bn; the upside is $5.5–7bn.

These are forward-looking estimates for the coming year, not reported 2026 results. The ranges assume no repeat of the large defence-focused round. The probabilities are subjective planning judgments, not outputs from a statistical forecasting model.

06 / How should you prepare your next raise?

Raise for the next proof point.
Know what comes after it.

AgFunder’s global round medians were $1m at seed, $7m at Series A and $14m at Series B. Use them as reference points, then work backward from the milestone and cash required. They are not promised round sizes or US-only benchmarks. [2]

Your stageThe investor’s questionWhat the round should help establish
Seed
$1m reference		Does this work for a customer with a reason to buy?Performance in the intended setting, a clear buyer, paid demand or credible commitments, and a costed route to the next test.
Series A
$7m reference		Can this become a repeatable business?Repeat purchases or renewals, measurable customer value, understood delivery costs and a credible path to profitable sales.
Series B
$14m reference		Will more money scale what works?Sales beyond the founders’ network, reliable delivery and support, and evidence that expansion strengthens the economics.

The milestone guidance is an interpretation of the funding evidence. Hardware, biologicals and regulated products may need different validation periods and financing structures.

Make the Series A amount defensible.

An illustrative $5–8m ask can start the discussion around the $7m reference. Justify it with the work: customer expansion, field validation, lower delivery costs or a manufacturing step. Explain what will be demonstrably different when the money is spent.

Show repeat buying, not just interest.

Separate pilots from paid deployments, and first orders from renewals. Explain the customer’s result and your cost to deliver it. That makes the growth plan easier for an investor to assess.

What those round sizes mean for runway

Illustration: reserving 15% of a $7m raise leaves $5.95m. Over 24 months, that supports about $248,000 of average monthly net cash spending, before financing fees.

With a $1m seed raise, the same reserve leaves $850,000, or about $47,000 a month over 18 months. These are budgeting examples, not spending targets. If your necessary validation cannot fit, change the scope, funding amount or financing approach.

Your next step: write down the next funding milestone, the evidence it requires, its full cash cost and the plan if the following raise takes longer. Use the base case for the budget and preserve a workable downside. Let an improving market create options rather than rescue the plan.

Background / Why the market feels different

You are raising in a smaller market
than the boom years suggest.

Funding for farms, food production and related systems grew from $2.5bn in 2016 to $18.4bn in 2022, then fell to $9bn in 2025. Investors still fund the sector, but the peak is a poor reference for how much capital is available.

AgFunder calls this group “upstream.” It includes food science, biomaterials and agricultural finance, not just farm technology. Its share of broader agrifoodtech investment rose from 31% in 2021 to 56% in 2025 because other categories, including delivery, fell further. [1]

Appendix / Definitions and limits

How to read the numbers.

Why the two main datasets are not combined

AgFunder tracks the full food system. Its upstream group includes agriculture, food science, biomaterials and finance. PitchBook’s agtech category is narrower, but includes some companies that also serve other industries.

The report uses AgFunder to explain the decade and PitchBook to assess the current pace. A difference between their totals is not a measured change in the market. Dollar amounts are not adjusted for inflation unless stated.

How the scenarios were built

Each annual range is four times an assumed quarterly amount: $0.75–1bn in the downside case, $1–1.375bn in the base case and $1.375–1.75bn in the upside case.

The assumptions reflect new investment funds, cash returned from exits, financing costs and farm purchasing power. The 30% / 50% / 20% probabilities express subjective planning judgments. They are not measured historical frequencies, and the range boundaries are approximate.

No market-wide median round size, numerical deal count or US-only capital total is forecast. The available evidence does not support those estimates.

What remains unknown

The original nine-month 2025 PitchBook deal count has not been checked. Other gaps include a verified US-only 2026 funding total, a complete review of companies serving non-agricultural markets and a complete total for money raised by ag-focused funds in 2026.

The fund closes and acquisitions cited are examples, not a complete market tally. Acquisition prices and cash returned to investors are often undisclosed. US farm-income averages also hide large differences between customers.

DigitalFoodLab: additional context only

DigitalFoodLab reported $3.5bn of broad agrifoodtech funding in Q1 2026 and $3bn in Q2. That implies a $13bn annual pace in its own dataset. It is not used in the main forecast. [15]

Its September commentary described improving seed activity and a 64% funding share for agtech plus food science. Those observations come from one provider and cannot be compared directly with the other datasets. They do not establish a sector-wide recovery in seed funding. [16]
